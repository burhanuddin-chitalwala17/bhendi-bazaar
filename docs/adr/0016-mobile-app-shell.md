# ADR-0016: The phone storefront is an app shell — bottom tab bar, dense grids, page-level chrome

- **Date:** 2026-08-11
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Context:** [ADR-0015](0015-mobile-first-design.md) made the phone the primary device and fixed what was *broken* on it — unreachable buttons, hidden sign-in, trapped scroll, sub-40px targets. What it did not decide is what the phone storefront should *be*. Its rule 1 ("the base layout is single-column") was written against dashboards and admin tables, and taken literally it produced a storefront where a category listing showed one product per screen: a shopper cannot compare what they cannot see side by side, and scrolling past six full-width cards to reach the seventh is not how anyone buys clothes. Around that, the same desktop proportions were shipped verbatim to a 360px screen — a 40px display heading, a 160px-tall brand hero above the fold, `tracking-[0.32em]` overlines eating a third of a line, `gap-5` gutters between cards that were themselves only as wide as the gutters were generous.

  The chrome had the matching problem in the other direction. PR-59 restored mobile search and categories by giving them a *second header row*: 14px of app bar plus a 44px strip, ~100px of permanent chrome before any product, and still nothing that told a shopper where they were or how to get to the cart. Meanwhile every quick-commerce and marketplace app the store competes with for the same thumb — Zepto, Blinkit, Amazon, Meesho, Myntra — has converged on the same shape: a thin top bar that is mostly search, primary navigation docked at the bottom, and a dense multi-column grid. That convergence is not fashion; it is where the thumb is and how much a phone screen holds.

  There is also a horizon: the intent is to wrap these same pages in a native shell rather than build a second client. That only works if the web pages already behave like app screens — respecting the notch and home indicator, not flashing tap highlights, not bouncing on overscroll — because those are the tells that survive a WebView.
- **Decision:** On phones the storefront renders as an app shell, and layout density is set from the 360px screen up.
  1. **Bottom tab bar is the primary navigation.** `src/components/layout/mobile-tab-bar.tsx`, `md:hidden`, five destinations (Home, Categories, Cart, Orders, Account). The top bar shrinks to one row of logo + search + account, and everything the old second row carried moves *into the tab bar* — ADR-0015 rule 2 is satisfied by relocation, never by deletion. Categories opens a bottom sheet rather than a route, because a chooser is a modal act, not a place.
  2. **Products are 3-up on a phone, category lanes 2-up.** Declared once as `PRODUCT_GRID_CLASSES` in `src/components/shared/product-grid.tsx` and used by every listing (category, search, home lanes, similar). This is the explicit, scoped exception to ADR-0015 rule 1: a *comparison* grid is multi-column at base; everything else — forms, checkout, tables, detail pages — stays single-column, and rule 1 is unchanged for them.
  3. **A tile is designed at ~105px, not shrunk to it.** Anything that cannot survive that width is dropped below `sm` rather than truncated (the tracked-out category overline, the "SALE" word badge, the healthy-stock line), and what remains is re-sized rather than re-flowed: `text-[0.6875rem]` names, `gap-2` gutters, `p-2` content, a corner discount chip carrying what the word badge used to.
  4. **Page-level chrome is docked, not scrolled past.** The product page's Add-to-cart bar is fixed above the tab bar below `md` and returns to the flow at `md` — one element that changes position, not two copies of the same control.
  5. **The shell respects the device.** `viewportFit: "cover"` in the root viewport export, `env(safe-area-inset-*)` behind the `pb-safe`/`pb-tabbar`/`bottom-tabbar` utilities keyed to one `--tab-bar-h` token, no tap highlight, no overscroll bounce, no iOS text inflation. `Sheet` (`src/components/ui/sheet.tsx`) is the phone's dialog; `Dialog` stays the desktop's.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  - ***Keep ADR-0015 rule 1 absolute and stay single-column*** — the consistent reading, and the rule we were already under. Rejected because it optimises for a property (never scrolls horizontally, never crowds) that a product tile does not need: a tile is an image with a price, it survives 105px, and one-per-screen actively costs the shopper the comparison they came for. The rule was written to stop desktop tables being crammed onto phones; applying it to a product grid is following the letter against the reason. Hence a *named* exception with a boundary, rather than a quiet violation or a rewrite of ADR-0015.
  - *A second, phone-only set of components or routes* — full control per form factor, and the shape most competitor apps actually ship. Rejected for the same reason ADR-0015 rejected it: two UIs for one developer, now made worse by the WebView plan, which needs *one* set of pages that are already app-shaped rather than a third target.
  - *Keep the two-row header, skip the tab bar* — cheapest, and PR-59 had just built it. Rejected because it spends ~100px of a 640px screen on chrome and still leaves cart and account a dropdown away; the tab bar costs 56px at the far end of the screen, where nothing is being read, and puts five destinations one thumb-reach apart.
  - *A hamburger drawer for navigation* — the conventional web answer, one control instead of five. Rejected: it hides every destination behind a tap and a decision, and the cart badge — the one piece of state a shopper checks constantly — becomes invisible.
  - *2-up product grid on phones* — roomier tiles, less aggressive. Rejected as a half-measure that keeps the desktop card intact rather than designing a phone tile; 3-up is what the reference apps ship and it is what makes a category readable in one screen. Reversible: the column count lives in one constant.
- **Consequences:**
  - ✅ The phone layout is decided, not inherited. A new listing gets the right grid by using `ProductGrid`, and a new destination gets into navigation by being added to one array.
  - ✅ Column counts, tab-bar height, and safe-area maths each exist once, so the sticky CTA, the tab bar, and the page's bottom padding cannot drift apart.
  - ✅ The pages are WebView-ready without a second client: safe areas, no tap flash, no rubber-band, docked primary actions.
  - ⚠️ ADR-0015 rule 1 now has an exception, and exceptions get generalised by whoever meets them next. The boundary is written into rule 2 above — *comparison grids only* — and `/bb-review` has to hold it.
  - ⚠️ Every component that renders inside a tile is now responsible for surviving 105px, which is a real constraint on `PriceDisplay`, `StockStatus`, and anything added later. The size scales (`xs`) exist for it; a caller that ignores them will overflow.
  - ⚠️ Two navigation surfaces exist (tab bar below `md`, navbar from `md`), so a new destination has to be added in both or it is missing on one. Nothing enforces this — same standing gap ADR-0015 named, same answer: review, until a screenshot gate exists.
  - ⚠️ The tab bar occupies 56px on every storefront page, and any future fixed element has to sit above it via `bottom-tabbar` rather than `bottom-0`.
