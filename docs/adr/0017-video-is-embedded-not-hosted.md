# ADR-0017: Video is embedded from a third-party host, never stored in our own object storage

- **Date:** 2026-08-13
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Context:** Every asset the store serves — product photographs, category heroes, avatars — lives in Vercel Blob, uploaded through one path and referenced by URL. So when the catalogue needed video, the shape of the answer looked settled: put the file where the other files are. It is not settled, because video differs from a photograph by an order of magnitude in exactly the dimension that allowance is metered on.

  Two properties of the storage tier decide this. Transfer is metered **per account**, shared across every asset the store serves, and video is 15–20× the bytes of every photograph on a product page combined — so a few hundred plays a day spends the catalogue's entire monthly transfer on one feature. And exceeding the allowance does not produce a bill; it withdraws access to the store for thirty days. The failure mode of self-hosted video is therefore not "video stops working", it is "every product image in the store stops working, for a month, because someone watched a clip". A nice-to-have cannot be allowed to hold the storefront hostage.

  There is a second, independent force. Object storage serves one file at one bitrate to everyone. Most buyers here are on a phone on a variable connection — the premise of [ADR-0015](0015-mobile-first-design.md) — and a single 1080p MP4 does not degrade for them, it stalls. Adaptive delivery is not a refinement on top of hosting video; it is most of the work of hosting video, and it is not what object storage is.
- **Decision:** Video reaches the storefront as an embed, never as a file we serve.
  1. **No video file is uploaded to our own object storage, on any tier.** This holds regardless of plan: the bandwidth asymmetry and the absence of transcoding are properties of object storage, not of the free allowance. Photographs continue to upload to Blob unchanged — this decision is about video, not about assets in general.
  2. **A video is referenced, not stored**, by a provider-tagged pair — a `kind` and a `ref` — so the reference identifies a video on a host rather than pinning a URL on one host. Swapping hosts is then a renderer change and a backfill of `ref`, not a schema migration.
  3. **The player is not in the initial HTML.** Embeds are click-to-play against a poster derived from the reference, because a third-party player is several hundred kilobytes of script and the product page's paint budget belongs to the product.
  4. **This applies to every future video surface** — category, home hero, reviews, anything later. A new surface inherits the decision; it does not get to re-open the storage question on its own behalf.
  5. **If self-hosting is ever genuinely wanted**, the replacement is a video service (Cloudflare Stream, Mux) or zero-egress object storage (Cloudflare R2) — never general-purpose blob storage sitting behind the same allowance as the images.

  First applied by [product-video](../specs/product-video/) — see its [trd.md](../specs/product-video/trd.md) D1, D2, D9 for how this lands in that feature.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  - ***Upload video to Vercel Blob like everything else*** — the conventional answer and very nearly the default one: the upload path exists, the hostname is already allow-listed, no third party enters the system, and we keep full control of the player and the page. Rejected on the coupling: the video would spend the images' transfer allowance, and the penalty for overrunning is withdrawal rather than a bill, so the blast radius of a popular video is the entire catalogue going blank. A feature whose worst case takes down a working storefront is not worth its best case. The absence of transcoding rejects it a second time, independently.
  - *Blob, but in a separate store to isolate the quota* — the first mitigation anyone proposes, and the reason it is recorded here. It does not work: Vercel meters storage, operations, and transfer per account and states explicitly that usage is unaffected by how many stores exist. Separate stores partition *organisation*, not *quota*.
  - *Cloudflare Stream or Mux* — the correct tool, with transcoding, adaptive delivery, and per-minute pricing that would cost very little at this catalogue's size. Rejected for now rather than on merit: it is a paid service and a second vendor for a feature with no proven demand, and decision 2 means adopting it later is a backfill rather than a redesign. This is the option to reach for the moment embedding proves insufficient.
  - *Cloudflare R2* — 10 GB free with zero egress fees, which genuinely answers the bandwidth objection and would let us serve our own files. Rejected because it answers only that objection: R2 is object storage too, so there is still one bitrate for every buyer, and the mobile-connection problem is the half that is harder to fix.
  - *Self-host a single MP4 behind a native `<video>` element* — the simplest possible player story, no iframe, no third-party script, full styling control, and the right answer for a short silent loop. Rejected as the general solution for the same one-bitrate reason, and noted as the case that will legitimately come back: the boundary is bytes per view, not principle. A 2 MB muted loop is in photograph territory; a 30-second clip with audio is not.
- **Consequences:**
  - ✅ Video costs no storage and no transfer. The image allowance — the thing the storefront actually depends on — is untouched by however popular a video becomes.
  - ✅ Adaptive bitrate, a global CDN, and a player already tuned for a phone on a bad connection, none of which we build or operate.
  - ✅ The host is swappable by construction, so this decision can be revisited later at the cost of a backfill rather than a migration.
  - ⚠️ **We display content we do not control.** A video can be deleted, made private, age-restricted, or have embedding disabled by its owner at any moment, and we cannot detect the last of those before rendering. A visible "unavailable" state is therefore mandatory rather than an edge case, on every surface that embeds.
  - ⚠️ **A monetised third-party video carries its host's advertising onto our product page**, which can include a competitor's. This is accepted knowingly: the alternative is requiring orgs to own every video, which is a content-operations rule the store cannot actually enforce.
  - ⚠️ The player remains heavy third-party script. Click-to-play is the whole mitigation, so any later change that autoplays an embed, or mounts the iframe eagerly, silently spends the paint budget this decision protected.
  - ⚠️ Short silent autoplay loops are *not* served by this decision, and wanting one is a legitimate future request rather than a violation. It needs its own decision about bytes per view, not an appeal to this one.
  - ⚠️ This decision is about where video lives; it does not address the Hobby plan's own restriction to non-commercial use, which the store's use of that tier sits inside regardless of what it stores.
