| Q1 | What arrives from the shop's Business app, and how is it kept? | The Business app's contacts (name and phone): first Meta's initial sync after a coexistence connect, then each add, edit or removal from the `smb_app_state_sync` webhook, all arriving as Not yet (parent Q9). | Q5 | How do opt-in, STOP and HINDI work in a chat? | consent-core reads each inbound WhatsApp message as a neutral event. YES, HAAN or हाँ (alone, with or without the shop's code) opts the sender in for that shop on WhatsApp; STOP and its equivalents stop it (Q9); HINDI switches that shop's messages to Hindi. Only the buyer's own message counts, never an echo of the shop's. A YES from a number never seen makes a contact, opted in, source Chat, with the message as evidence. Each change gets one free reply inside the 24-hour window, in the buyer's language: on YES "You're in. <shop> will send you offers here. Reply STOP any time. Reply HINDI for Hindi.", on STOP "Done. <shop> won't send you offers. Reply YES to start again." If that reply fails, the change stands and the failure is logged | Claude · reversible | 2026-11-22 | Consent's words belong where consent is kept (parent Q108). was: the words were audience's and this subfeature only turned messages into events | decided |
| Q6 | What is a contact, and who counts as one? | One person known to one shop, keyed by (shop, WhatsApp business-scoped id) with the phone number when Meta gives it, or by (shop, phone in E.164) for one that came from an order or an upload, with an optional name and email (parent Q96). The same person at two shops is two contacts; nothing about one is visible to the other. Past buyers are read live from orders and shown as Not yet in the Buyers count, never stored as contacts until they opt in | Claude · reversible | 2026-11-21 | One shop's audience is its own; Meta may withhold the number. was: keyed by (shop, phone in E.164) only | implemented (unit 1) |
| Q7 | What does a consent record hold? | Sender (a shop, or the platform: Bhendi Bazaar's own consents sit in the same table, with no platform list or screen until platform sending, parent Q27), channel (WhatsApp or email), state, source, when, and evidence (the order id, the inbound message id or the attestation id). States are Opted in and Stopped; Not yet is the absence of an opt-in, never a stored row. Changes are appended, never overwritten, so the history shows every opt-in and stop | Claude · reversible | 2026-11-06 | DPDP and Meta both ask "when and how did they agree"; a history answers it | implemented (unit 1) |
| Q8 | How does audience reach checkout, orders, wishlists and the WhatsApp send without importing them? | Through jobs and public services only. Checkout records the tick on the order and, on payment confirmed, enqueues one shared job, `audience.checkout-opt-in` (order id, shops, phone, email, the box's wording version); checkout never imports audience. Orders and wishlists are read through checkout's and wishlist's public services ("buyers of shop X since a date or in a category", "wishlisters of shop X's products"), returning phones, never addresses. Towards sending, audience answers `may(sender, contactId, channel)` (consent-core D2) and emits `audience.consent-changed`; it never counts sends, the caps staying with the WhatsApp send in campaigns | Claude · reversible | 2026-11-06 | The stock-changed pattern (ADR-0038); "side effects belong to the transition that earns them" (checkout CLAUDE.md). was: `consentFor(shop, phone, channel)` | decided |
| Q9 | What stops consent, and can it restart? | STOP, UNSUBSCRIBE, बंद or रोको sent alone on WhatsApp to a shop (trimmed, case-folded) stops that shop's WhatsApp consent; the email unsubscribe link stops that sender's email consent; neither touches another sender or channel (parent Q9). A stopped contact may opt in again by any opt-in source, the history keeping both, but an uploaded list can never re-opt-in someone who stopped | Claude · reversible | 2026-11-06 | Exact words only, so "stop sending the wrong size" is a chat, not an opt-out; Hindi per parent Q37 | decided |
| Q10 | How long is consent kept, and what does a deletion request remove? | Consent history is kept three years after its last change. A person's deletion request removes their contact at every shop holding it, with its list memberships and consents, leaving only a minimal "stopped" record (sender, channel, hashed phone, date) so a later upload cannot silently re-add them | Owner | 2026-11-06 | Proof if Meta or a buyer disputes consent | decided |
| Q11 | Can a shop message the phone contacts synced at connect? | Once each, ever, with a first offer that asks for consent in the same message: an offer or product with buttons "Yes, keep me posted" and "Stop messages". Tapping Yes or replying YES (HAAN, हाँ) opts them in to that shop; Stop or STOP stops them; any other reply is a conversation, not consent; no reply leaves them Not yet, never sent another. Synced contacts qualify, those uploaded from a file too (audience Q15), and each newly synced contact once it arrives; it counts as a marketing message (cost, spending limit, how-often rule) and goes in batches of up to 100 a day | Owner | 2026-11-23 | A plain request for consent is mostly refused; an offer with the question in it earns a yes. Once-ever and batches protect the shop's number quality, since Meta asks for opt-in before marketing | decided |
| Q12 | What happens to contacts when WhatsApp is connected again? | A connection made again through Meta's pop-up (after the app was moved or reinstalled, or we were removed) runs the contact sync again, if Meta allows it for the new onboarding, which the TRD spike confirms. Known contacts are matched by number or business-scoped id and keep their consent; new ones arrive Not yet, each eligible for its one first offer; no one gets a second. Renewing our access alone runs no sync; live changes resume | Owner | 2026-11-23 | A reconnect should catch up the phone book without resetting anyone's choice | decided |
